The map redraws itself as you descend

Rendering 503 names at once puts a quarter of the index under 2px. Instead, a group stays a single block until its children are big enough to tell apart, then dissolves. Scrub the slider and watch blocks turn into tickers — this is the real rule running, not an animation.

Layout runs once

layoutMap() is memoised on the data. A pinch mutates a Skia transform, so squarify never re-runs mid-gesture. That is what keeps it at frame rate with the full market loaded.

The tail folds, it doesn't vanish

Names too small to ever be tapped collapse into a +N tile that opens as a list. Tests assert all 503 stay reachable — folding hides a tile, never a company.

Shape is guarded, not just size

A 26×1 sliver clears any area floor and is still a hairline. A group refuses to dissolve when its children would be worse than 8:1, taking the worst case from 103:1 to 7.3:1.

Honest caveats

What you are actually looking at

The geometry, colours and level-of-detail decisions come from the app's real squarify.ts, buildTree.ts and color.ts, run against a 503-name index: 99 real large-caps with approximate market caps, padded out with synthetic names on a realistic cap curve. The prices are therefore fictional; the layout, grouping, folding, colour mapping and dissolve behaviour are exactly what ships.

  • Device chrome is drawn in CSS here. In the app it is native — the phone frames on this page are for context only.
  • The real canvas is Skia, not SVG. Same geometry and same culling thresholds; SVG is just what a web page can show you.
  • Gestures can't be shown on a page. Focal-point pinch, clamped pan and double-tap-to-reset need a touchscreen; the slider stands in for the zoom half.
  • Not affiliated with Finviz. No Finviz data, code or branding is used — see the licensing note in the README.
