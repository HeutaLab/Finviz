Zoom is the whole product

The desktop map scales to fit and the tickers become specks. Here the layout is computed once into a fixed world space; zoom only changes a transform, and labels are culled by their on-screen size. Scrub the slider — this is the real culling rule running live.

Layout runs once

layoutMap() is memoised on the data. A pinch mutates a Skia transform, so squarify never re-runs mid-gesture. That is what keeps it at frame rate with the full market loaded.

Labels appear as you go

A tile narrower than 26 px on screen draws nothing. Past 54 px it earns a second line with the percent change. Zooming reveals information rather than just magnifying it.

Tiles stay square

Squarified layout keeps the worst aspect ratio under 6:1 in tests. Slice-and-dice would produce 100:1 slivers — unreadable, and far too thin to tap.

Honest caveats

What you are actually looking at

The treemap geometry and every colour are produced by the app's real squarify.ts, buildTree.ts and color.ts, run against the deterministic mock dataset (99 real large-caps with approximate market caps and seeded price moves). The prices are therefore fictional; the layout, grouping, colour mapping and label-culling behaviour are exactly what ships.

  • Device chrome is drawn in CSS here. In the app it is native — the phone frames on this page are for context only.
  • The real canvas is Skia, not SVG. Same geometry and same culling thresholds; SVG is just what a web page can show you.
  • Gestures can't be shown on a page. Focal-point pinch, clamped pan and double-tap-to-reset need a touchscreen; the slider stands in for the zoom half.
  • Not affiliated with Finviz. No Finviz data, code or branding is used — see the licensing note in the README.
