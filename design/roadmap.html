Nuclear Sub Roadmap

Roughly 8–12 months of solo work, or 4–5 months for three people. But the first decision isn't engineering at all — and if you get it wrong, none of the rest can pay for itself.

The wall

Real-time data is priced per user, per month

Exchanges charge for redistributing real-time quotes to end users — a recurring fee for every subscriber you have, on top of the vendor's own bill. That single fact sits directly on top of the $1.70 you net from a $2 subscription.

Delayed data carries no per-subscriber exchange fee — which is precisely why every free app offers it. Going real-time also brings obligations that cost time rather than money: a redistributor agreement per feed, monthly subscriber reporting, entitlement enforcement, and audit rights over your user records.

This is the decision the whole roadmap hangs off. Every feature below is buildable. Only some of them are buildable at $2 a month. Pick the lane before writing another line of code.

Pick a lane

Three coherent products

Recommended
$2/mo

Best delayed map on any platform

Keep the 15-minute tier. Sell depth instead of speed: every universe, every timeframe, the time scrubber, screener-driven maps, widgets, alerts. Nobody has built a genuinely great delayed map on mobile, and most retail users cannot act on 15 seconds anyway.

Break-even ~21 subscribers. Data cost stays flat as you grow.

$12–20/mo

Real-time professional tool

Consolidated tape, streaming, depth. A different product for a different customer, and the price has to carry both the per-user fee and the compliance overhead. Viable, but it competes with entrenched desktop tools rather than with a website that's bad on phones.

Break-even moves to roughly 4–6× the subscriber count.

$2–5/mo

IEX real-time, labelled honestly

Genuinely live, but a single venue rather than the consolidated tape, so prints and volumes differ from what a broker shows. Workable if the UI is explicit about it. Gets ugly the first time a user compares your number to their brokerage and finds a gap.

Cheap, and a support burden you cannot design away.

The build

Seven phases, gated in this order

Sizing assumes one experienced engineer. The order matters — each phase removes a constraint the next one needs gone.

1

Make it survive 500 names

2–3 weeks blocks everything

Measured on a realistic 503-name universe: 6% of tiles get a label, 2% reach Apple's 44pt touch minimum, and 25% render under 2px. The layout is correct and fast — the product is unusable at that density.

  • Tail aggregation. Below a minimum area, collapse an industry's smallest names into one "+37 more" tile that opens on tap.
  • Level of detail. Sector-only at low zoom, industries next, tickers last. Detail arrives as you descend rather than all at once.
  • Minimum touch target. No tile smaller than 44pt is ever the deepest hit result; taps fall through to the parent.
2

Render the whole US market

3–4 weeks ~6,000 tiles at 120Hz
  • Skia Atlas for tiles instead of one React element per rect — thousands of quads in a single draw call.
  • Pre-rasterised glyph atlas for labels. Measuring text per tile per frame is the ceiling you hit first.
  • Static picture layer. Geometry only changes on universe or timeframe change; a price tick should recolour, never re-layout.
  • Animated colour transitions so live ticks read as movement rather than flicker.
3

A data spine you can trust

3–5 weeks correctness, not features
  • Streaming transport. WebSocket diffs applied to a resident snapshot; REST polling only as the fallback.
  • Corporate actions. Splits and dividends must adjust historical returns, or your 1Y numbers are quietly wrong. Cheap vendors get this wrong constantly.
  • Point-in-time constituents. A historical map built from today's index members is survivorship-biased and flattering.
  • Halts, delistings and dual listings handled explicitly rather than silently dropped.
4

The five features people would switch for

10–14 weeks the actual product
  • Time scrubber. Drag through the session and watch the map evolve. Needs intraday snapshot storage server-side. Nobody does this well on mobile — this is the one that gets screenshotted.
  • Relative-to-index colouring. On a red day everything is red and the map says nothing. Colour by performance relative to the index or sector and rotation appears. Days of work, disproportionate payoff.
  • Screener-driven maps. Define a filter, map the result. This is the actual moat of the site you're competing with.
  • Breadth panel. Advance/decline, percent above 50 and 200-day, new highs and lows. Context the treemap alone cannot carry.
  • Event overlay. Earnings today, ex-dividend, halted — marked on the tile.
5

Live on the whole platform

8–11 weeks retention, not acquisition
  • Home screen widgets. Watchlist heat, sector strip. The cheapest daily-active-user mechanism on iOS.
  • Live Activity in the Dynamic Island while the market is open.
  • Push alerts on percent moves, sector rotation and breadth thresholds — evaluated in the worker cron, not on device.
  • iPad and Mac. A treemap wants a big screen; this is where the product is genuinely better than the phone version, not merely bigger.
  • Watch complication, App Intents, Spotlight, iCloud watchlist sync.
6

Rigor

3–4 weeks currently the weakest area
  • Worker tests. The paywall is the most business-critical code in the repo and has zero coverage today. Miniflare covers it.
  • Golden-image tests on the map. Layout regressions are visual; a diff on a rendered canvas catches what unit tests cannot.
  • Accessibility. The canvas is one opaque element — a screen reader gets nothing at all. Needs a parallel accessible list, Dynamic Type, and a VoiceOver audit. This is an App Store review risk as well as an exclusion.
  • Sentry, performance traces, staged rollouts, feature flags, funnel analytics. You cannot tune a conversion rate you cannot see.
7

Compliance and trust

calendar, not dev time start early
  • Exchange agreements if you go real-time — signed per feed, with subscriber reporting and audit rights. Months of lead time, so start before you need it.
  • Display obligations. Attribution, timestamps, and limits on redistributing derived values.
  • Rename off "Finviz." The repo name and any store copy implying affiliation is trademark exposure that has nothing to do with your code.
  • Real privacy policy and terms that match the "no data sold" promise, plus a deletion request path.
Restraint

What not to build

Full charting. A year of work to lose to TradingView. Deep-link out instead — already wired.

Broker integration or order routing. A regulatory undertaking, not a feature. It changes what kind of company you are.

Social feeds and comments. Needs a scale you will not have early, and brings moderation obligations immediately.

Every asset class at once. Crypto, forex and world equities each carry their own data contract and symbology. Win US equities first.

If you only do three things

The short path

1 · Fix the 500-name wall

Without it the core claim is false at the scale you advertise. Two to three weeks, and everything else assumes it.

2 · Ship relative-to-index colouring

Days of work. Turns a map that says "everything is red" into one that says who is winning. The best effort-to-payoff ratio on this page.

3 · Get a real data quote

Ask two vendors what per-user redistribution actually costs at your expected scale. That number decides your price, and your price decides the roadmap.
